STUDIO NOTE · MID-AUTUMN 2026

The moon we share, and the osmanthus that smells like home.

Every year, when the fifteenth night of the eighth lunar month arrives, the workshop pauses. The moon is at its roundest, the osmanthus is in bloom, and somewhere a table is set with mooncakes and tea. For a manufacturing house rooted in China but serving salons and brands across the UK, Europe and far beyond, it is a night about distance — and about how small things keep us close.

Studio note: A Mid-Autumn reflection on the full moon, osmanthus and reunion — and what two Tang poems about the moon say about working with partners across oceans.

A festival of the round moon

Mid-Autumn falls on the full moon closest to the autumn equinox. In Chinese it is 中秋节 — the middle of autumn. Families gather, share round mooncakes, and step outside to look up. The roundness matters: a full moon and a round cake are both shapes of 团圆, reunion — the wish that the people who belong together are, if not in the same room, at least under the same sky.

“海上生明月，天涯共此时”

The Tang poet Zhang Jiuling wrote: “The bright moon rises above the sea; though we are far apart, we share this same moment.”

For us it lands close to home. The salons we supply in Manchester, the brands we develop for in Paris, the distributors we ship to across oceans — none of them are in the same room as the workshop. But on a night like this, the moon is the one thing none of us has to ship, and the one thing we all already share.

It is a useful reminder in a business built on long distances: the relationship is not only the container and the invoice. It is also the knowing that, somewhere, a partner is looking up at the exact same light.

The scent of osmanthus (桂花)

Mid-Autumn smells of 桂花 — osmanthus. Tiny golden flowers, almost invisible, that perfume the whole street. People steep them into wine, press them into sticky cakes, float them in tea. The fragrance is the season.

There is a lesson in it for anyone who makes product. Osmanthus is not the headline. It is the small, almost unnoticed detail that tells you a place is cared for. That is how we think about the quiet parts of hair work — the rooted blend at the tip, the way a weft lies flat, the shade that actually matches the client in the chair. No single detail sells the piece. Together they are the difference between “fine” and “this is the one I reorder.” Like osmanthus, the detail is what makes a house feel like home.

“露从今夜白，月是故乡明”

The Tang poet Du Fu wrote, amid separation: “The dew turns white from tonight; the moon of my homeland is brightest of all.”

It is a line for everyone who has left somewhere — and a great many of the salon owners and entrepreneurs we work with are exactly that: building a business in a second language, far from the street they grew up on.

We feel it too. A workshop that has spent nearly two decades learning the craft now sends its work to strangers who become regulars. The moon of the hometown may be brightest, but the one you stand under with people you trust runs a close second.

Reunion, and the work we do together

团圆 is usually drawn as a family around one table. We would widen it a little. The table also includes the buyer who finally approved a reference after three samples, the stylist who reordered the same blonde for two years, the brand founder who started with one development run and now fills a shelf. Reunion, to us, is the moment an order comes back — not as a transaction, but as a return.

So on this mid-autumn night we are grateful for the round moon, the osmanthus, and the partners who let a small manufacturing house on the other side of the world become part of their own tables and their own businesses.

Wherever you are under this moon

If you are celebrating tonight — with family, with friends, or quietly with a mooncake and a thought of home — we hope it is a good one. And if you are building a hair brand or a salon assortment abroad and would like a manufacturing partner who treats the small details as seriously as the shipment, we are happy to talk. The moon is free to everyone; the conversation is easy to start.
